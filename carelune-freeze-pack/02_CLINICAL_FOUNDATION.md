# Carelune Clinical Foundation V1

## Evidence hierarchy

Carelune clinical content should be governed in this order:

1. Indian law, professional scope, and government guidance;
2. current Indian stroke/rehabilitation guidance and local PM&R review;
3. high-quality international stroke guidelines;
4. validated assessment manuals and peer-reviewed evidence;
5. Carelune pilot observations, clearly labelled as local operational evidence;
6. expert consensus, explicitly documented when stronger evidence is unavailable.

India’s Ministry of Health stroke guidance describes rehabilitation as multidisciplinary, including physicians, nurses, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech therapists, psychologists, orthotists, dietitians, and social workers. [MoHFW stroke guidance](https://www.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Guidelines%20for%20Prevention%20and%20Managment%20of%20Stroke.pdf)

NICE likewise recommends a specialist multidisciplinary team, explicit responsibilities, caregiver training, a named care coordinator/key worker, a safe home environment, shared goals, and rehabilitation that continues while it helps the person achieve goals. [NICE NG236](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng236/chapter/Recommendations)

## What is frozen

- medically stable adult stroke is the first cohort;
- the referring neuro-physiotherapist remains Lead Physiotherapist;
- each discipline owns its assessments and interventions;
- one named coordinator owns operational continuity and closure;
- the care plan is goal-led, patient-specific, versioned, and auditable;
- caregiver workload must be minimised;
- direct visits remain available when virtual/caregiver-guided delivery is unsuitable;
- emergencies are routed to emergency services, not managed as Carelune support tickets.

## What is not frozen until clinical sign-off

- exact eligibility/exclusion rules;
- assessment instruments and licensed use;
- assessment frequency;
- exercise, OT, speech, swallow, nutrition, and psychology content;
- progression/regression thresholds;
- escalation thresholds and response targets;
- claims of effectiveness.

## Clinical team architecture

### Core programme roles

- Lead Physiotherapist
- Recovery Care Coordinator
- Rehabilitation Nurse
- Medical Clinician
- Clinical Operations Lead

### Governance/on-indication roles

- PM&R Specialist
- Occupational Therapist
- Speech and Swallow Therapist
- Dietitian
- Clinical Psychologist or appropriately qualified mental-health professional

Other referrals can include neurologist, orthotist/prosthetist, social worker, diet/feeding specialists, and local emergency/hospital services.

## Three-layer care architecture

### Layer 1 — Governed pathway/template

Contains evidence source, version, approving discipline, inclusion/exclusion rules, precautions, contraindications, stop conditions, escalation rules, and review date.

### Layer 2 — Patient-specific interdisciplinary plan

Contains goals, responsible discipline, approved intervention, dosage/frequency, delivery mode, assistance, equipment, precautions, review date, and rationale.

### Layer 3 — Caregiver daily schedule

Contains only what the caregiver needs: priority, action, simple instructions, who approved it, what to report, when to stop, and how to request help.

## Intervention delivery modes

- caregiver-guided home task;
- live virtual professional session;
- recorded video for professional review;
- direct home visit;
- clinic visit;
- hold pending reassessment.

The relevant professional selects the mode. Virtual delivery must never be the default when direct assessment is clinically necessary.

## Content-library governance

Every content item should store:

- discipline and clinical owner;
- functional goal/domain;
- intended population and capability prerequisites;
- instructions and caregiver role;
- dosage fields that must be personalised;
- precautions, contraindications, and stop conditions;
- media language/version;
- evidence source;
- author, reviewer, approval status, expiry/review date;
- whether video upload or live observation is required before progression.

AI may help tag, translate, caption, search, rank, and draft content. AI may not independently approve, assign, progress, regress, or stop an intervention.

## Clinical domains

- mobility, balance, transfers, gait, positioning, range and physical capacity;
- upper-limb function and activities of daily living;
- communication and cognition;
- swallowing, feeding safety, and oral care;
- medical stability, medication reconciliation/administration record, and secondary prevention;
- nutrition and hydration;
- mood, adjustment, behaviour, and caregiver wellbeing;
- participation, home environment, equipment, work/community reintegration;
- recovery barriers and social needs.

## Safety language

Carelune supports structured execution and routing during published service hours; it is not continuous clinical monitoring or an emergency service. Any high-risk swallowing, mobility, medication, psychological, or medical content must require explicit discipline-specific approval.

## Clinical governance before Patient 1

Dr. Farhan and the PM&R/discipline leads must approve:

- patient eligibility and exclusion checklist;
- escalation matrix and emergency language;
- first protocol/template version;
- every pilot content item;
- documentation minimums;
- clinical review schedule;
- adverse-event and readmission workflow;
- measurement set and permissions/licences.
